/ Technology

The instruments behind the outcomes

Each tool earns its place by making treatment more predictable or more comfortable. Here is what we use, what it replaces, and what it means for you.

CBCT 3D IMAGING

CBCT 3D Imaging

What it is
A cone-beam CT scanner that produces a low-dose, three-dimensional image of your jaw, teeth, nerves, and sinuses.
What it replaces
Flat 2D X-rays that cannot show bone volume or the exact position of nerves.
Patient benefit
We see exactly where your nerve and sinus sit before we begin, so implants are planned around your anatomy — not estimated during surgery.
DIGITAL INTRAORAL SCAN

Digital Intraoral Scanner

What it is
A handheld wand that captures a precise digital model of your teeth in a few minutes.
What it replaces
Putty impression trays and the gag reflex that comes with them.
Patient benefit
No impression goop, faster appointments, and restorations that fit accurately the first time.
GUIDED IMPLANT SURGERY

Guided Implant Surgery

What it is
A surgical guide, 3D-printed from your scan, that directs each implant to its planned position and depth.
What it replaces
Freehand placement that relies on the surgeon's estimate during the procedure.
Patient benefit
More predictable results, smaller incisions, and placement accurate to a fraction of a millimeter.
SAME-DAY CERAMIC MILLING

Same-Day Ceramic Milling

What it is
An in-house mill that carves a ceramic crown from a solid block while you wait, designed from your digital scan.
What it replaces
Two-week lab turnarounds and temporary crowns that fall off.
Patient benefit
Many crowns are designed, milled, and fitted in a single visit — one appointment instead of two.
